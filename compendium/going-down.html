Moment 1 of 10

A cord, a ladder, a bean-stalk

In the first age the way between heaven and earth was a thing you could climb. Tibet’s first king came down it by a cord and a ladder, and the Iroquois woman was pushed through the hole where the tree of light had stood. A Latvian singer climbed into heaven on a bean-stalk. The way worked in both directions: Homer’s gods go to the far Ethiopians and come back on the twelfth day.

The crown before the king

In the records that keep kings, the office existed before anyone held it, and its regalia lay in heaven. Kingship is the thing that comes down, and the kings are where it lands. The Buddhists alone elect their first king, and pay him in rice.
