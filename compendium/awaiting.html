Moment 1 of 21

For seventy generations in the valleys of the earth

The losers of the first age were bound rather than destroyed, and almost every binding has a term. Enoch’s angels are to be bound fast for seventy generations in the valleys of the earth, till the day of their judgement, and 2 Enoch says they are condemned to be under the earth till heaven and earth are ended. In Enoch’s vision the stars that did not rise at their appointed time are bound for ten thousand years. The Zohar keeps Aza and Azael in the mountains of darkness, where those who seek them are taught sorceries, and Mujāhid, in al-Ṭabarī’s commentary, hangs Hārūt and Mārūt upside-down in chains of iron.

The giants’ prison

Moment 3 of 21

His time hath not yet come

Iran’s dragon-king Zahhāk, Azi Dahak in the old books, was bound and not killed. When Faridun would have struck, the angel Surush cried, Strike not, his time hath not yet come, and Faridun chained him in a narrow gorge of Damāvand. Al-Ṭabarī says the Persians claim he is there to this day, fettered with iron, and they kept the day he was tied up as a feast. At the nine-thousandth year, says the Bahman Yasht, he breaks loose, and the hero Keresaspa sleeps in the plain of Peshyansai under the glory of heaven, guarded by a myriad spirits, so that when the dragon is unfettered he may arise and slay him.

Moment 13 of 21

Our years are bound

In Mexico every fifty-two years the count of the years was bound up, and every fire was put out and drilled anew. On the night of the new fire everyone was in very great fear, for if no fire could be made the sun would not rise and the demons would come down to eat them. Pregnant women were masked with maguey and shut in the granaries, and children were kept awake lest they turn into mice. When the fire was drilled on a captive’s breast the towns blazed until it seemed to be day, and Sahagún’s informants said the count of the years was very old, invented by Quetzalcoatl.

Once a year

Some bindings come round on the calendar. Quintus says the river by Memnon’s grave turns to blood once a year on the day he died, and at Thebes his broken statue gave out a sound at dawn. The reeds that grew where Midas’s barber buried his master’s secret repeated it about twelve months later, and the birds of Memnon war again each time the sun has passed through the twelve signs. Hesiod times the harvest by the rising of the Pleiades.

In a year called One Reed

Some wait to return rather than to break loose. Quetzalcoatl left saying he would come back in a year called One Reed, to visit them when it was time for the world to end, and in another telling he burned himself by the eastern sea and his heart rose as the morning star. A priest of Babylon who wrote his country’s history in Greek. The book is lost; its lines survive in extracts made by Alexander Polyhistor, Apollodorus, Eusebius and Syncellus, printed in Cory’s Ancient Fragments of 1832.

"After the death of Ardates, his son Xisuthrus reigned eighteen sari. In his time happened a great Deluge; the history of which is thus described. The Deity, Cronus, appeared to him in a vision, and warned him that upon the fifteenth day of the month Desius there would be a flood, by which mankind would be destroyed. He therefore enjoined him to write a history of the beginning, procedure, and conclusion of all things; and to bury it in the city of the Sun at Sippara; and to build a vessel, and take with him into it his friends and relations; and to convey on board every thing necessary to sustain life, together with all the different animals, both birds and quadrupeds, and trust himself fearlessly to the deep. Having asked the Deity, whither he was to sail? he was answered, "To the Gods:" upon which he offered up a prayer for the good of mankind." "Xisuthrus then paid his adoration to the earth: and having constructed an altar, offered sacrifices to the gods, and, with those who had come out of the vessel with him, disappeared. They, who remained within, finding that their companions did not return, quitted the vessel with many lamentations, and called continually on the name of Xisuthrus. Him they saw no more; but they could distinguish his voice in the air, and could hear him admonish them to pay due regard to religion; and likewise informed them that it was upon account of his piety that he was translated to live with the gods; that his wife and daughter, and the pilot, had obtained the same honour. To this he added, that they should return to Babylonia; and, as it was ordained, search for the writings at Sippara, which they were to make known to all mankind: moreover that the place, wherein they then were, was the land of Armenia." "The vessel being thus stranded in Armenia, some part of it yet remains in the Corcyræan mountains of Armenia; and the people scrape off the bitumen, with which it had been outwardly coated, and make use of it by way of an alexipharmic and amulet. And when they returned to Babylon, and had found the writings at Sippara, they built cities, and erected temples: and Babylon was thus inhabited again."
