PThe Horse's Foot

At Van, a city on a lake in eastern Turkey, a tall niche is cut into the cliff; an old kingdom there, Urartu, made it, and the Armenians call it Mher's door. Their epic of Sasun says its last hero, Little Mher, sits behind it with his horse. His father cursed him to be "deathless, heirless", and once a year, on the eve of Ascension, the Christian feast forty days after Easter, the door opens and he rides out to try the ground, and it will not bear him: "Hay-hoy, it is in vain: the earth too has grown old, it will not hold under my horse's feet." Far older, the Mahābhārata, the Sanskrit epic of India's great war, says the Earth herself went up to the gods because the kings on her were too many: "This earth, the bearer, could not bear herself by herself." An ancient commentator's note on Homer's Iliad, which preserves a few lines of a poem now lost, the Cypria, says the Earth asked Zeus to be lightened, and gives the lost poem's answer: Zeus "resolved to relieve the all-nurturing earth of men by causing the great struggle of the Ilian war". Ilian means Trojan. The question of this volume is how the ground came to be so heavy, and who is waiting for it to hold.

Told by the Armenian epic of Sasun (Krpo's telling recorded 1873; the curse is in the later collated text); the Mahābhārata (India); the scholion on Iliad 1.5 and the Cypria (Greece).

IThe Elders

Many peoples' oldest records tell of someone before their gods, and in most of them he is thrown down. The Norse say a frost-giant, Ymir, was there first, and Snorri Sturluson, the Christian Icelander who wrote the old stories down in the thirteenth century, refuses him the title: "By no means do we acknowledge him God". The Hittites, who ruled central Turkey more than three thousand years ago, kept a song of three kings of heaven, each thrown down by the next. It begins "Once in the olden days Alalus was king in heaven." Nine years, and Anu threw him down; nine more, and Kumarbi threw down Anu; and the Hittite kings went on swearing their treaties before the losers, the olden gods. The Greeks tell it the same way: Ouranos the Sky thrown down by his son Kronos, and Kronos by his son Zeus. Philo of Byblos, a Phoenician writing in Greek about AD 100, matched his own country's gods to those names himself: "Elos, that is Kronos, trapped his father Ouranos". And Plutarch heard that Kronos, deposed by Zeus, sleeps on an island beyond Britain, "for his sleep has been devised as a bondage for him". The elder is thrown down, and kept.

What becomes of the elder's body is the world. Babylon's creation poem begins with two waters, Apsu and Tiamat; their young god Marduk killed Tiamat and "split her in two, like a fish for drying" to make the sky and the earth, as the Norse say the earth was made "Out of Ymir's flesh". Sumer's King List gives its first kings reigns of tens of thousands of years and closes the age in one line, "Then the flood swept over." Other records put someone before mankind rather than before the gods: the Maya Popol Vuh has a false sun, Seven Macaw, shining before the true one; Islam has the jinn, who held the earth before Adam. Genesis puts nothing before God.

IIGoing Down

In the first age the way between heaven and earth stood open; that open way is what this volume calls the door. The records say what came down it. Sumer's King List states it as a fact of the calendar: "After the kingship descended from heaven, the kingship was in Eridug." The Babylonian poem of Etana says the signs of kingship, "Scepter, crown, headdress, and staff", lay before the sky god in heaven before any king on earth had them. The Book of Enoch, a Jewish book of the centuries before Christ, says two hundred angels came down on Mount Hermon in the days of Jared, an early patriarch before Noah, and that the mountain got its name from the oath they swore there, because its name sounds like the Hebrew for a binding curse: "they called it Mount Hermon, because they had sworn and bound themselves by mutual imprecations upon it." The Ethiopian Kebra Nagast has the angels ask to be sent: "put us to the test so that Thou mayest know whether we are able to keep Thy word". Tibet's Old Chronicle says its first kings came down from heaven and went back up a cord when they died, and an inscription of about AD 800 has the first of them come "to rule over the land of men for seven generations". In the North the god Rig came down and lay with human wives, and to his son, the first earl, "runes he taught him". Japan's Nihongi says that when the gods came down to settle the land and subdued its powers, "the only one who refused submission was the Star-God Kagase-wo." The Buddhist Aggañña Sutta tells the descent as a loss of light: at the start of a world, beings come down from the World of Radiance, "made of mind, feeding on rapture, self-luminous, traversing the air", and one of them "tasted the savoury earth with his finger", and they grew coarse and lost their shining. And Homer's gods still went down to feast with "the blameless Ethiopians" at the edge of the world.

IIIThe Gifts of the Smiths

Everything that makes a civilisation came through the door, and the records disagree about whether that was a gift or a crime. The smith is the figure they agree on: the one who works metal, and is worshipped for it or bound for it. Babylon's list of sages names seven who came before the flood, the first "Uʾana, who carries out the ordinances of heaven and earth perfectly"; Berossus calls him Oannes and says he taught men everything. Of Adapa, the sage of Eridu, the same tradition says the god gave him wisdom and withheld the rest: "To him he granted wisdom, eternal life he did not grant him." Greece's Prometheus stole fire for men and was chained to a mountain for it. In the Book of Enoch the teaching is the crime the angels are charged with: Azazel "made known to them the metals", and in the Apocryphon of John, a book of the Gnostics, Christians of the second century who held that this world was made and run by lesser powers they called the rulers, the rulers' angels "brought them gold, silver, gifts, and metals of copper, iron, and every sort." Philo's Phoenicians had two brothers who "discovered iron and how to work it", and worshipped one of them after he died; their inventor of writing, Taautus, Philo matched to Egypt and Greece in one breath: "the Egyptians called him Thoyth, the Alexandrians Thoth, and the Greeks Hermes." A Jewish writer in Greek of the second century BC, preserved by Eusebius, made the same kind of match for the star-teacher: "Atlas is the same as Enoch". Iran's Book of Kings has King Tahmuras bind the demons, who "begged their lives" and bought them by teaching him writing: "He gave them quarter / To learn their secret." Mexico's chroniclers remember Quetzalcoatl, who came and taught, "enseñándoles por obras y palabras el camino de la virtud", teaching them by works and words the way of virtue. The same arts, the same teachers: honoured in one file, bound in the next.

IVHybrids

The beings who came down did not stay teachers. Genesis says "the sons of God saw the daughters of men that they were fair; and they took them wives of all which they chose", and that the mighty men of old were born of it; the Ethiopian Kebra Nagast says of those children, "they became giants, whose height reached unto the clouds". Philo's giants were so large that their names "were given to the mountains over which they ruled". In Greece the goddess of love used to boast of making the gods lie with mortal women, until on Mount Ida she lay with a Trojan herdsman herself and lost the right to boast: "I mated all the immortals with mortal women", she says, and now her mouth will have no more power among the gods. Troy was the most mixed house of all: its royal line was half divine, and even its wall was god-built; the sea-god, who served Troy's king for a year, says "I verily built for the Trojans round about their city a wall". Iceland later married the North into that house: Snorri says a grandson of Priam "had a child named Tror, whom we call Thor", who wedded "the prophetess that is called Sibil, whom we call Sif". Hesiod, the Greek poet of about 700 BC, counts the mixed generation as an age of its own: after the bronze men Zeus made "a god-like race of hero-men who are called demi-gods, the race before our own". In India the five Pāṇḍava brothers, the heroes of the great war, were begotten on mortal women by gods. China remembers the age as a confusion: "People and spirits were mixed together and could not be told apart." Ovid, a Roman poet, says the Earth gave life to the giants' spilt blood and "shaped them into the form of men". Plato tells the mixing as a whole kingdom: on the island of Atlantis the sea god Poseidon fathered ten kings on a mortal woman, Cleito, and their line kept its virtue for generations, until "the divine portion began to fade away, and became diluted too often and too much with the mortal admixture, and the human nature got the upper hand". The children of the mixing are the heroes and the giants, and the next part is the weight of them.

VThe Burden

Then the earth could not carry it. Babylon's flood story begins with population: "The land had become great; the people had multiplied." The noise of them keeps the gods awake, and the chief god, Enlil, sends plague, then famine, then the flood; before the end, "Enlil’s tears flowed down". Genesis gives a cause the others do not: "the wickedness of man was great in the earth", and the earth was "filled with violence". India says the Earth went up because the kings born on her were too many and too violent; its appendix, the Harivaṃśa, says the earth breaks under good kings too, and that "the killing of the kings must be done, to settle the earth's burden". Ovid gives the Earth a voice: scorched when the sun's chariot ran wild, she "lifted up her all-productive face as far as her neck" and asked Jupiter what she had done to deserve it. In Iran a dragon-king begs the river goddess for the opposite boon, "that I may make all the seven Karshvares of the earth empty of men", and is refused; and King Yima, told the earth is full, enlarges it three times instead. The Greek Cypria says Zeus sent the Trojan War "that the load of death might empty the world". Plato's Critias has the council itself: "Zeus, the god of gods, who rules according to law", seeing the kings of Atlantis gone bad, calls the gods together to speak, and the dialogue breaks off before he does; the verdict is lost. Every archive records the weight; what differs is the cause: number in Babylon and wickedness in Genesis, and in India the kings themselves.

VIRemedy

The first remedy was water. Babylon's flood hero, Utnapishtim, is warned by a god, builds a boat and lives; the Gilgamesh epic has him tell how the chief god blessed him afterwards: "Enlil came up into the boat, / he took hold of my hands and brought me out." Berossus, who told it in Greek with Greek names for Babylon's gods, has "The Deity, Cronus, appeared to him in a vision, and warned him"; Cronus is the Greek spelling of Kronos, standing here for a Babylonian god. Genesis has Noah. Plato's Atlantis, its kings judged, sank "in a single day and night of misfortune". The Maya Popol Vuh has a first, failed mankind carved of wood: "A flood was planned by Heart of Sky that came down upon the heads of the effigies carved of wood".

The second remedy was war, and the records say so themselves. In the Babylonian poem of Erra, Marduk, king of the gods, admits what the first time cost: "Once, in my anger, I arose from my throne, causing a flood;" and when he leaves his throne a second time the god of plague rules in his place. The Sibyl, a prophetess of Greek legend in whose name Jews and Christians wrote, says God held the ocean back from the Titans, the elder gods who fought heaven, "Because he promised that again no flood / Should be brought upon men of evil soul." The Roman poet Statius has Jupiter ask, "Am I ever to be spent in punishing the wicked?" So the Greek war at Troy and the Indian war at Kurukṣetra, the Mahābhārata's battlefield, stand where the flood stood. In India the war is an older one carried on: the asuras, beaten in heaven, were born on earth as kings, and the gods followed them down into human families to fight them there. Hesiod names who the Greek wars destroyed. Of the demigods, "Grim war and dread battle destroyed a part of them, some in the land of Cadmus at seven-gated Thebe" and "some, when it had brought them in ships over the great sea gulf to Troy for rich-haired Helen’s sake"; the rest Zeus set "apart from men" at the ends of the earth. A fragment of the poem later attributed to him, the Catalogue of Women, gives the reason in one sentence: Zeus "was meditating marvellous deeds", "declaring that he would destroy the lives of the demi-gods, that the children of the gods should not mate with wretched mortals". The Cypria says why the war was sent; Hesiod says whom it ended. Virgil, the Roman poet of Troy's fall, has the goddess lift the cloud from Aeneas's eyes so that he sees who is pulling the city down: "the gods, the relentless gods, overturn this wealth and make Troy topple from her pinnacle." The Sibyl disputes it. She says Troy's poet, "False writer and of doubtful native land", will tell of Ilium "not truthfully, indeed, / But skillfully". One file puts the gods on the field; the other calls that a poet's invention. A quieter remedy runs beside the war: send a portion of heaven down. In the Mahābhārata the gods are told each to send a part of themselves down as a human, to oppose the demon-kings weighing on the earth, "be born in her, by portions, severally, for opposition"; and the Mongolian Geser sends the king of heaven's son down to the oppressed earth. In Mexico the gods themselves die at Teotihuacan so that the sun will move; the Sun's demand is "Porque pido su sangre y su reino", because I ask for their blood and their kingdom. Sahagún's Aztec informants told how the light was first made there: a god nobody counted, covered in sores, was told "sé tu el que alumbres bubosito", be the one who gives light, little one with the sores, and he leapt into the fire and rose as the sun.

VIIThe Cut

Read together, the records suggest why the door was shut: destruction thinned the burden but did not end what caused it, and separation could. The archives do not say this in one voice; the joining is ours ◇. What they do say is that the door was shut. China's Book of Documents says God on high sent two officers, Chong and Li, "to make an end of the communications between earth and heaven; and the descents (of spirits) ceased". The family that did the cutting, says the Guoyu, "lost their office and became the Sima clan", and from that clan came Sima Qian, who wrote China's history. Tibet's kings had gone up to heaven on a cord until one king cut his own and became the first to leave a tomb. Enoch carries the sentence to the angels who came down: "you shall not ascend into heaven unto all eternity". The Hittite song has the gods who "severed the heaven from the earth with a cleaver". Sumer's gods swear after the flood that no human will live for ever, "after the assembly had made the Flood sweep over to destroy the seed of mankind". Babel is the cut made by language: Genesis says the LORD came down to see the city and the tower and confounded the tongues, and in Mexico the lord of the heights says of the tower at Cholula, "vení y confundámoslos", come and let us confound them. The goddess of love, who had mixed gods and women, loses her power: "now my mouth shall no more have this power among the gods". The Māori tell the cut as the first event of all: Heaven and Earth, Rangi and Papa, lay locked together in the dark, and their son Tane-mahuta, father of the forests, set his head on his mother and his feet against his father, and "Now are rent apart Rangi and Papa", crying out "Why commit you so dreadful a crime as to slay us, as to rend your parents apart?" The door was not shut everywhere at once; at the rim of the world, where Homer's gods still dined with the Ethiopians, it stayed ajar.

VIIIClaimed

After the shutting, the shut door has two files: the kings who claim they came through it, and the books that say who really did. The earliest king we know of who was declared a god ruled in Mesopotamia about 2250 BC: the city of Agade petitioned eight gods to make Naram-Sin a god, and they built him a temple. A century and a half later Šulgi of Ur, who took the same title, asks in his own hymn, "What mortal has ever reached the heavens?" The claims meet courts. Alexander, wounded, tells his friends that what flows from him is blood, not the ichor of the gods; Herod Agrippa, hailed as a god, says "I, whom you call a god, am commanded presently to depart this life"; in Seneca's satire a senate of gods votes the deified emperor Claudius out of heaven "within thirty days", one god complaining that "it was a great thing to become a god; now you have made it a farce." In Isaiah the LORD says there is no god beside him; the Gnostic books, which held that this world was made by a lesser, blind power, turn the line round and put it in that maker's mouth, "I am God and there is no other God beside me", and have his own mother correct him: "Do not lie, Ialdabaoth". It is a reversal of Isaiah, not a parallel to it. The name this volume's companion poem is about comes from the same chapter of Isaiah, the fall of a king of Babylon told as the fall of a star. In the Hebrew he is הֵילֵל בֶּן־שָׁחַר, Helel son of Dawn; the Greek Bible makes him ὁ ἑωσφόρος, the dawn-bringer; the Latin makes him Lucifer; and the King James Bible prints the name with a capital, once: "How art thou fallen from heaven, O Lucifer, son of the morning!" In Peru a servant came back "to preach that he was Viracocha", the creator god, and the people saw that it was false. Arabia's record is cut in stone. Esarhaddon, king of Assyria, took the gods of the Arabs captive, and gave them back on his own terms: he restored "the gods of the Arabs, and I inscribed the might of the god Aššur, my lord" on them, with his own name, and set over the Arabs a woman, Tabūʾa, raised in his father's palace. At Petra the Nabataeans made a king a god; the Greek geographer Stephanus of Byzantium records the place ὅπου Ὀβόδης ὁ βασιλεύς, ὃν θεοποιοῦσι, τέθαπται, where Obodas the king, whom they make a god, lies buried, and a Petra inscription names "the banqueting-society (marzeah) of Obodat the god." In Yemen the great dam of Maʾrib, which fed a kingdom, broke under the Christian king Abraha, and his inscription on the repair opens by invoking Rḥmnn, the Merciful, his Messiah and the Holy Spirit, before it turns to the sluices and the stone. An older Sabaean king had cut a rival city's water "by order of ʿAthtar". Who holds the gods holds the water.

The losers' side of the story survives because its books travelled. The Book of Giants, which tells the fallen angels' and their sons' side, was banned by the church and still went from the Dead Sea caves to the followers of Mani, a Persian prophet of the third century, and a copy was made at Merv in central Asia. The Popol Vuh was written down in hiding after the Spanish conquest, "its witnesses and those who ponder it hide their faces". The Buddhist account of the first king gives the opposite of a god-king: he is elected. "Chosen by the whole people, Vāseṭṭha, is what is meant by Mahā Sammata", the Great Elect, and the Buddha adds that kings' "origin was from among those very beings, and no others". Every claimant in this part gets the rank, and not the life.

IXAwaiting

The losers of the first age were bound rather than destroyed, and almost every binding has a term. Enoch's angels are to be bound "for seventy generations in the valleys of the earth, till the day of their judgement". Iran's dragon-king Zahhāk, whom the old books spell Azi Dahak, hangs chained in Mount Damāvand; at the nine-thousandth year, says the Bahman Yasht, he breaks loose, and a sleeping hero is kept against that day, "when Azi Dahak [Zohak] becomes unfettered (arazhak), he may arise and slay him". The Norse "bound Loki", the trickster god, and his son the wolf, until the end of the world. China's stone monkey, Sun Wukong, who stole immortality from heaven, lies under a mountain five hundred years, fed on orders: "When he is hungry, give him iron pellets; when thirsty, molten copper." The Maya bury the earthquake-giant Cabracan, who swore "I will wreck them as long as there is sun and light". The Mandaeans, a small faith of Iraq whose maker of the world is a lesser power like the Gnostics' blind ruler, set him in the stocks "bis zum Ende der Welten", until the end of the worlds.

Some doors to deathlessness were closed by a single failure. The Māori say Maui, the trickster demigod, tried to pass through the body of Hine-nui-te-po, the great lady of the night, and failed: "If that goddess had not been deceived by Maui-tikitiki, men would not have died, but would in that case have lived for ever". In Mexico every fifty-two years the fire was put out and drilled anew, and on that night "todos tenian muy grande miedo", everyone was greatly afraid, because if no new fire could be made the world would end. Some wait to return rather than to break loose. Mexico's Quetzalcoatl left saying he would come back "en un año que se llamaría ce ácatl", in a year called One Reed; the Hopi elder brother was to keep a stone tablet "and bring it back with him when he hears the desperate call for aid." Germany's emperor Barbarossa sleeps in a mountain and wakes to ask "Do the ravens still fly round the mountain?"; Armenia's Mher waits for the ground to hold. The Bible keeps the same rule of a term: Revelation binds Satan for a thousand years and then looses him for a little season. And some stayed alive through all of it: Utnapishtim, Enoch, and Fintan of Ireland, the witnesses who cannot die, and the Sibyl's voice runs from the flood to Rome. The Hermetic Poimandres gives the rule of every binding: "The period being ended, the bond that bound them all was loosened by God's Will."

  1. 01 It will not hold under my horse’s feet2 writings · 9 lines

    Armenia’s epic of Sasun says its last hero waits in the rock. David cursed his son Little Mher to be deathless and heirless, and gave him the Raven’s Rock for his place until the world is destroyed and built again, so that the ground can hold him. Russia’s giant Svyatogor carries the same weight: the damp mother earth would not bear him, and lifting a bag that held the earth’s pull he sank to his knees, blood instead of tears on his face. He breathes his strength into Ilya, who refuses more: I have strength enough, or the earth will not carry me.

  2. 02 This earth, the bearer, could not bear herself7 writings · 30 lines

    The Mahābhārata says the Earth herself went up to the gods because the kings born on her were too many: this earth, the bearer, could not bear herself. Once no child died and the whole earth was filled thickly with living things, and when Yama, god of death, stopped killing at the gods’ sacrifice, the creatures multiplied past bearing. The Harivaṃśa’s Earth comes worn out by the burden like a ship, and Brahmā names the remedy, the taking-down of the burden: the kings must be killed to settle it. Al-Bīrūnī carried the plan into Arabic, that when the earth has too many inhabitants its ruler sends a messenger to reduce the number.

  3. 03 To relieve the all-nurturing earth11 writings · 18 lines

    Greece says the same of its war. The Cypria has Zeus see the countless tribes of men oppressing the earth and resolve in pity to relieve her by the great struggle of the Ilian war. The old note on Homer that keeps those lines adds that Earth herself asked to be lightened, and that Momos gave Zeus his counsel. Euripides’ Apollo says the gods made deaths to draw off the overflowing fullness of mortals, and Achilles, sitting by the ships, calls himself a profitless burden upon the earth.

  4. 04 The earth cries out ✦11 writings · 18 lines

    Elsewhere the Earth speaks for herself: Ovid’s Earth, scorched, lifts her face and complains to Jupiter, and in Nonnus she begs Zeus to look on the pains of the downcast world. In the Hermetic Virgin of the World the elements come before God one after another, and Earth last of all, in bitter grief. In Enoch the earth cries up to the gates of heaven, and in the Book of Giants it rises against the Watchers and raises a complaint. Mani’s world-bearer, the Omophorus, tires under the weight and trembles, and the Dogon’s Amma found the earth grown impure and unfit for his reign.

  5. 05 How the ground came to be so heavy7 writings · 25 lines

    The question is how the ground came to be so heavy, and the oldest answer is number and noise. Babylon’s Atra-ḫasīs says twelve hundred years had not yet gone by when the people grew many and the god was troubled by their clamour, and plague and famine came year by year before the flood. In Japan the land below is loud with gods that buzz like flies, and Izanami swears to strangle a thousand of the living every day. In Iran Yima, told the earth is full, enlarges it three times, and the dragon Azi Dahaka begs the river goddess for the boon of emptying all seven regions of men.

  6. 06 Out of order ✦9 writings · 20 lines

    China remembers a time out of order, when people and spirits were mixed together and the oppressed cried out to heaven. In Yao’s time the waters ran out of their channels, and ten suns came out together and scorched the grain. In Egypt Ra summons the elders in secret so that mankind will not see and lose heart, and the children of Nut, says the Book of the Dead, made war and raised tumult until their years were shortened. The Buddhists date it to the first hoard, when the fields were divided and one being stole another’s plot.

  7. 07 The land stops bearing ✦9 writings · 11 lines

    Sometimes the burden shows as a land that stops bearing: when the Hittite god Telipinu walked away in anger, mist seized the windows and smoke seized the house. In Phrygia a pestilence fell and the land ceased to bear fruit, and the Swedes, after years of famine, offered up their king. Hesiod’s Earth groaned under the children Heaven hid inside her, and Kumarbi, who swallowed his father’s manhood, is told a burden has been laid inside him. At Ugarit the gracious gods are born with one lip to the earth and one to the heavens, and birds and fish go into their mouths.

  8. 08 Once a year the door opens ✦7 writings · 27 lines

    Some doors open on a calendar. In Mexico the years were bound every fifty-two, every fire was put out, and on the night of the new fire everyone was greatly afraid that the sun would not rise again. Memnon’s river runs with blood once a year on the day he died, and his birds war again whenever the sun has passed the twelve signs. Dawn rises every morning from the bed of Tithonus, and the reeds that grew over Midas’s secret spoke it after twelve months.

  9. 09 Bound till the day of their judgement ✦12 writings · 19 lines

    Enoch’s angels are bound fast for seventy generations in the valleys of the earth, till the day of their judgement, and the stars that did not rise at their appointed time are bound for ten thousand years. 2 Enoch has three of them go down to Hermon and be condemned to lie under the earth till heaven and earth are ended.     Ireland says a slaughter is not usual without a tale-bearer escaping from it, and Tuan says, That man am I. Fintan lay a year under the Flood in a cave above Tul Tuinde, and God kept him alive till the time of the saints so that he could relate the Takings of Ireland. Banba says she is elder than Noah, and the Sibyl says she was the bride of Noah’s son. Others carried writing across: Xisuthrus buried the writings at Sippar, and John Cassian says Ham carved the forbidden arts on plates of metal the water could not destroy.

  11. 11 The people made again ✦4 writings · 12 lines

    After the water someone had to live on the earth. Sumer says the kingship descended from heaven again, to Kiš, and that Inana sprinkled the water of life on those who had stood in the face of the flood. In Mexico the gods asked who would dwell on the earth now that the sky had stood still, and Quetzalcoatl went down to the land of the dead for the precious bones, which were ground and bled upon so that the vassals of the gods were born. The History of the Britons, written about 829, says the sea covered those who attacked a tower in the sea, and from the family of one ship all Ireland was filled.

  12. 12 The second remedy was war11 writings · 16 lines

    Babylon’s poem of Erra has the confession: Marduk, king of the gods, says that once, in his anger, he arose from his throne and caused a flood, and the seams of heaven and earth were rent. When he rises a second time the plague-god Erra rules in his place, and the Seven who march with the storm-god are called his deluge. The Sumerian Lugal-e calls Ninurta’s war a deluge, and in the Cuthaean Legend a deluge is decided in heaven while another rises from the depths. The Māori say the war of the winds against their brothers drowned a great part of the dry land.

  13. 13 Set against one another ✦8 writings · 12 lines

    War does the flood’s work most thoroughly when it turns a people on itself. Erra’s decree on the nations runs Sealander against Sealander, Assyrian against Assyrian, land against land, man against man, brother against brother. Genesis Rabbah has God say he will entangle them, these with those, and in Enoch’s vision one of them gives a sword to the elephants and camels and asses and they smite each other. India’s kings, driven by Time, kill one another, as the Cimmerian kings did who chose to die in their own land; the Narts of the Caucasus, offered eternal life or eternal glory, chose glory and each dug himself a grave.

  14. 14 No second flood6 writings · 15 lines

    Heaven, having used the flood once, is said to have bound itself against using it again. The Sibyl says that when the Titans resolved to fight the starry heaven, God checked the ocean’s waters, because he promised that again no flood should be brought upon men of evil soul. Ovid’s Jupiter remembers that the world is fated to burn at the end, so he sends water first, and Statius has him weary of both: Am I ever to be spent in punishing the wicked? Plato’s gods hesitate to destroy the double men because the sacrifices would end, and in the Mandaean Ginza the powers say that where the sword does not reach these people, fire will, and where fire does not, water.

  15. 15 Troy stands where the flood stood9 writings · 22 lines

    Greece says why the war was sent. The Cypria has Zeus see the countless tribes of men weighing on the earth and in pity resolve to relieve her with the great struggle at Ilium, so that the load of death might empty the world. Apollodorus gives another reason, that the race of the demigods might be lifted away, and Euripides’ Apollo says the gods made deaths to bail the outrage of mortals out of the earth. Inside the war the Dawn threatens to leave the world to blind night for her son Memnon, and quails before Zeus.

  16. 16 Kurukṣetra, an older war carried on3 writings · 12 lines

    India sets its great war where the flood stood, and remembers an older one behind it: the gods’ war on the daityas lasted thirty-two thousand years and made the earth a single ocean of blood. Before Kurukṣetra the Earth comes to the gods’ hall to collect, and on the field Kṛṣṇa tells Arjuna, I am Time, grown ripe, the destroyer of worlds; they are already slain by me. Afterwards Yudhiṣṭhira counts the dead down to the unmarked heroes, and the sea takes Kṛṣṇa’s city the moment Arjuna turns back. Al-Bīrūnī, writing in Arabic about 1030, explains that when the earth has too many inhabitants its ruler sends a messenger to reduce the number.

  17. 17 Grim war destroyed a part of them4 writings · 8 lines

    Hesiod names whom the Greek wars ended: the god-like race of hero-men called demigods, some at seven-gated Thebes and some at Troy for Helen’s sake, while Zeus set the rest apart from men at the ends of the earth. The Catalogue of Women says Zeus was meditating marvellous deeds, to destroy the lives of the demigods so that the children of the gods should not mate with mortals. The Iliad itself calls the dead by the Achaean wall the race of half-divine men, and Aeschylus’ Prometheus says Zeus meant to make the race of mortals vanish and plant a new one.

  18. 18 The gods overturn Troy2 writings · 6 lines

    Virgil lets Aeneas see it. Venus lifts the cloud from his eyes and shows him who is pulling the city down: the gods, the relentless gods, overturn this wealth and make Troy topple from her pinnacle. Servius adds that the dread shapes appear once Venus has gone. Aeneas goes down guided by a god through fire and foes, and over Ida the day-star is rising as he carries his father out.

  19. 19 False writer, and of doubtful native land7 writings · 15 lines

    The Sibyl, who says she left the long walls of Babylon to proclaim God’s wrath to the Greeks, calls Troy’s poet a false writer of doubtful native land, who will tell it not truthfully but skilfully. Others doubted Helen: Stesichorus recanted, that story is not true, you did not reach the citadel of Troy. In Euripides Hera gives Paris a breathing image made of sky while the real Helen waits in Egypt. When the image goes up into the air the soldiers ask whether it was for a cloud they toiled, and India says the same of Sītā: a woman of illusion was made for the killing of Rāvaṇa.

  20. 20 A portion of heaven sent down13 writings · 28 lines

    The quieter remedy sends heaven down in pieces: the Mahābhārata’s gods are told to cast off the Earth’s burden by being born in her, by portions, severally, for opposition, and in the Rāmāyaṇa Viṣṇu sets his mind among men to kill Rāvaṇa, whose boon had left men out. The Mongolian Geser is the son of the king of heaven, sent when the earth’s five hundred years have run out, and he asks for his father’s throne before he goes. In the Hermetic Virgin of the World the Earth complains and Osiris and Isis are sent down for a little while. China’s archer Yi was sent down with a red bow to relieve the hardships of the lower earth, and the goddess Ba, sent down to stop the storm, could not go back up.

  21. 21 A child given to the sea ✦5 writings · 5 lines

    Some remedies are paid with one life. Poseidon sent a flood and a monster on Ethiopia because the queen vied with the Nereids, and Ammon foretold deliverance if her daughter Andromeda were bound out for the monster; Ovid calls Ammon unjust. Joppa, founded, they said, before the Flood, kept the monster’s bones until Scaurus carried them to Rome, more than forty feet long. Philo of Byblos says it was the custom of the ancients in great danger to give up the most beloved of their children as a ransom.

  22. 22 Their blood and their kingdom3 writings · 9 lines

    In Mexico the gods paid with themselves: when the new sun and moon stood still, the gods asked how they could live, and resolved to die so that the sun would rise again through their death. The wind killed them all; Xolotl fled into the water as the axolotl and was killed there. In the Legend of the Suns the Sun says, Because I ask for their blood and their kingdom, and there was a slaughter of gods at Teotihuacan. The History of the Mexicans by their Paintings says that fourteen years after the flood the gods made a sun to eat hearts and drink blood, and made war to feed it.

  23. 23 Be the one who gives light1 writings · 22 lines

    Sahagún’s Nahua teachers told how the light was first made: before there was day the gods met at Teotihuacan and asked who would light the world. Tecuciztecatl offered himself and only precious things, and for the other they chose a god nobody counted, covered in sores, who offered green reeds and the scabs of his sores. Four times the proud one ran at the fire and turned back. Nanahuatzin closed his eyes and leapt, and the gods who looked to the east saw him rise as the sun, with the moon level beside him until a god struck its face with a rabbit.

  1. 01 An end of the communications between earth and heaven5 writings · 10 lines

    China kept the moment in its oldest book of documents: the people’s cries reached heaven, and God surveyed them and smelled no fragrance of virtue, only the rank odour of punishments; he sent Chong and Li to make an end of the communications between earth and heaven. A king of Chu later asked what that meant, and was told that in ancient times the people and the spirits did not mix. When they came to be mixed until nothing could be told apart, Zhuanxu set Chong over heaven and Li over earth so that neither side trespassed on the other. Another telling has one lift heaven while the other presses down earth, and when Gonggong fought Zhuanxu he broke the pillar of heaven, so that the sky still tilts to the north-west.

  2. 02 The severers became the historians4 writings · 11 lines

    The family that did the cutting kept the record of it. Under Zhuanxu Chong managed heaven and Li managed earth, and their line held the office down through Xia and Shang. Under King Xuan of Zhou they lost the charge and became the Sima clan, hereditary keepers of the Zhou histories, and Sima Qian of that clan chose the most refined words of the old accounts and set them at the head of his book. Phoenicia has its own first recorder: Philo of Byblos says Taautus, whom the Egyptians call Thoth, was the first to think of letters and to write records, and the seven sons of Sydyk and their eighth brother first set these things down at his order.

  3. 03 The cord cut2 writings · 5 lines

    Tibet’s first king came down from the sky by the dmu-cord and the dmu-ladder, and the Bonpos said that one who comes that way is a god. The kings went back up the same way until the groom Lo-ngam asked that King Drigum’s cord be cut and his ladder turned down, and the king granted it. Drigum was killed, and his body was put in a hundred copper vessels set mouth to mouth and sent down the Tsang river. The Chronicle then sets down the funeral rite to be kept for evermore.

  4. 04 You shall not ascend into heaven11 writings · 20 lines

    Enoch carries the sentence to the Watchers who came down: from henceforth you shall not ascend into heaven unto all eternity. Azazel is bound hand and foot and cast into the darkness in Dudael, and the Zohar says the two angels sit in chains of iron in the mountains of darkness. Mani’s Kephalaia says the prison was made before the Watchers ever fell, and the Book of Giants keeps Enoch’s tablets, sent to Shemihazah and his companions. In the Cave of Treasures the children of Seth who go down from the holy mountain shall never again ascend it, and in China the goddess Ba, sent down to stop the storm, could not go back up.

  5. 05 Ra goes up on the Cow ✦3 writings · 11 lines

    Egypt remembers a time when Ra was king and mankind and gods were still united. After the slaughter Ra says his body is weak for the first time and his heart too weary to remain with them, and he has his daughter Nut, the sky, take him up on her back, leaving Thoth in his place as his vicar. Herodotus was told that gods once ruled in Egypt, not living together with men, and that in all the reigns since no god had appeared in human form. Tutankhamun’s Restoration Stela says the gods had turned their backs.

  6. 06 The last of the heavenly ones leaves ✦12 writings · 21 lines

    Elsewhere the gods leave of their own accord: Ovid’s Astraea, last of the immortals, abandons the blood-soaked earth, and Hesiod says Shame and Indignation will forsake mankind for the company of the gods. Enoch’s Wisdom went forth to dwell among the children of men, found no dwelling-place, and returned to take her seat among the angels. In Plato’s Statesman the pilot of the universe lets go the helm and forgetting fills the world, and the Hermetic Asclepius foresees the gods hastening back from earth to heaven, leaving Egypt widowed. India says that on the day Kṛṣṇa went to heaven the Kali age came down, and in Japan the elder god Ōkuninushi gives the visible world to heaven’s grandson and withdraws to govern the hidden, where he hides for ever.

  7. 07 Severed with a cleaver5 writings · 8 lines

    The Hittite Song of Ullikummi remembers the parting as the work of a tool. The giant Ubelluri, on whom heaven and earth were built, did not notice when the gods severed the heaven from the earth with a cleaver, though now his right shoulder is a little sore, and the olden gods are asked to bring out the ancient copper cutter again. In Babylon Marduk splits Tiamat like a fish for drying and sets half of her up as the sky, and Berossus has Belus cut the woman Omoroca asunder into earth and heaven. Greece remembers a parting made at a meal, at Mecone, where gods and mortal men were being divided and Prometheus cut up an ox for them.

  8. 08 No man will live for ever9 writings · 16 lines

    Sumer’s gods swore after the flood that no human would live for ever: Enki recalls the oath made after the assembly had made the Flood sweep over to destroy the seed of mankind. Adapa, the sage of Eridu, was given wisdom but not eternal life, and was warned that before Anu he would be offered the food and water of death. Genesis sets the cherubim and a flaming sword to keep the way of the tree of life, and Enoch says no mortal may touch the fragrant tree till the great judgement. The Māori say death came into the world when Maui failed to pass through the body of the great ancestress Hine-nui-te-po, who flashes where the horizon meets the sky, because a little bird laughed.

  9. 09 The glory leaves the first king ✦5 writings · 10 lines

    Iran tells the closing as a loss of glory. In Yima’s reign flocks and men were undying and waters and plants undrying, with neither cold wind nor hot; then the Glory left him in the shape of a bird, and the commentators say it departed three times. The Gāthās already name his sin, and the Bundahishn tells of the first couple’s lie. King Kai-Us built seven dwellings in the Alburz, one of gold, two of silver, two of steel and two of crystal, where the decrepit were made young again, and he tried to reach the sky.

  10. 10 Come and let us confound them15 writings · 25 lines

    Babel is the cut made by language: Genesis says the LORD confounded the language of all the earth there, and the Sibyl says winds cast down the tower and set men wrangling with each other. In Mexico an old man of Cholula, a hundred years of age, told a friar that the lord of the heights said of the tower, Come and let us confound them, for it is not right that those of the earth, living in the flesh, should mix with us. Before the parting, says Sumer’s spell of Nudimmud, the many-tongued lands spoke to Enlil as one. India’s gods won Speech away from the asuras, who were left crying barbarous words, and Ireland kept the tradition that Hebrew was the tongue before the Tower and will be the tongue after doomsday.

  11. 11 One star under two names ✦11 writings · 12 lines

    The morning star and the evening star are one planet, and the writings keep the slow finding-out. Diogenes Laertius gives the discovery to Pythagoras or to Parmenides, and Cicero sets out the names: Phosphoros in Greek and Lucifer in Latin when it goes before the sun, Hesperos when it follows. The Chinese ode names a star of the east and a star of the west, and James Legge, putting them into English as Lucifer and Hesperus, noted that the poet took them for two. Egypt called Venus the star of the ship of the Bennu-Osiris, and in 1892 Frege used the evening star and the morning star to show how one thing can carry two senses.

  12. 12 The same gods under other names ✦26 writings · 34 lines

    Once the tongues were parted the gods went on under other names, and some writers kept the lists. Herodotus gives the Scythian names of the Greek gods, Hestia as Tabiti and Zeus as Papaios, and the treaty Ramesses II and Ḫattušili swore before a thousand gods of Hatti and a thousand gods of Egypt writes the Hittite sun-goddess as Re of Arinna. Kanishka’s stone names his gods in Bactrian with an Indian gloss, and Prometheus calls his mother Themis or Earth, one form with many names. Milton has the fallen angels blotted out of heaven’s books until they got them new names among the sons of Eve.

  13. 13 No more power among the gods6 writings · 10 lines

    The goddess of love, who had mixed gods and women, loses her power. In the Homeric Hymn, Aphrodite says on Ida that until now the gods feared her jibes and the wiles by which she mated all the immortals with mortal women, but now her mouth shall no more have this power among the gods. The Catalogue says Zeus resolved to destroy the demigods, so that the children of the gods should not mate with mortals. Al-Ṭabarī tells of the Iranian king Hūshang, who subdued Iblis and his armies and forbade them to mix with human beings, writing the covenant on a white sheet, and a Russian song says that since the heroes ran into the stone mountains there have been no heroes in Holy Russia.

  14. 14 Rangi and Papa rent apart1 writings · 11 lines

    The Māori tell the cut as the first event of all: Heaven and Earth, Rangi and Papa, lay locked together, and their children lived in the dark between them until they took counsel; the fiercest said kill them, and Tane-mahuta, father of the forests, said rend them apart. He set his head on his mother and his feet against his father, and Rangi and Papa were rent apart with cries and groans. Only Tawhiri-ma-tea, father of winds and storms, refused, and he went up to his father to make war on his brothers. Their love goes on: Earth’s sighs rise to Heaven as mist, and his tears fall on her as dew.

  15. 15 Watchers on the boundary ✦11 writings · 17 lines

    Once the worlds were parted the line between them needed keepers: Hesiod puts the Titans behind a fence of bronze as far below the earth as the earth is below heaven, an anvil’s fall of nine days and nights. In the Edda the gods build a citadel round the world against the giants, and break their oaths to the giant who built their wall. A clay tablet from Pylos begins, Thus the watchers guard the coast. Kashmir is guarded by Nīla, lord of the Nāgas, who would not live among men, and in Estonia the heavenly sages set Kalevipoeg at the gate of hell, his hand fettered to the rock, keeping the other prisoners in their chains.

  16. 16 At the rim it stayed ajar4 writings · 8 lines

    The door did not shut everywhere at once. The Hesiodic Catalogue remembers when the feasts and the seats were shared between the deathless gods and mortal men, and Homer’s gods still went to feast with the Ethiopians, the farthest of men. Herodotus heard of a Table of the Sun outside the Ethiopians’ city, a meadow of meat that the earth brought forth of itself. In the Ethiopic Alexander Romance the people of Meroe climb a mountain they call the paradise of their gods, to sacrifice there at the start of each year, and in Japan an earthly god comes to meet heaven’s descending grandson and offers to go before him.

  1. 01 Agade asks the gods to make its king a god5 writings · 9 lines

    The earliest king known to have been declared a god ruled at Agade about 2250 BC. Because Naram-Sin protected the foundations of his city, its citizens asked eight gods, from Aštar in Eanna to Nergal in Kutha, that he be made the god of their city, and they built him a temple within Agade. The Curse of Agade remembers him rising like daylight on the holy throne and then trying to alter Enlil’s word. A century and a half later Šulgi of Ur called himself the god of all the lands, and his own hymn asks what mortal has ever reached the heavens.

  2. 02 Blood, not the ichor of the gods9 writings · 12 lines

    The claims met their courts. Plutarch’s Alexander, wounded, tells his friends that what flows from him is blood, not the ichor of the gods, and Arrian says his share in divinity did not rest on his mother’s word. Athens hailed Demetrius as a present god, not made of wood or stone, and Herod Agrippa, hailed as a god, said that he whom they called a god was commanded presently to depart this life. The First Emperor of China counted his line forward to ten thousand generations, and the Qurʾān’s Pharaoh proclaims, I am your Lord the Highest.

  3. 03 A senate of gods votes him out5 writings · 10 lines

    Rome made its gods by decree and unmade one by satire. Livy has Romulus hailed as a god, the son of a god, and seen descending from heaven at dawn; Suetonius has Caesar numbered among the gods by formal decree. A star appeared at the games for Caesar, which Augustus declared his father’s, and a former praetor swore he saw Augustus himself go up to heaven. In Seneca’s satire a senate of gods votes Claudius out of heaven, one god complaining that once it was a great thing to become a god, and now they had made it a farce.

  4. 04 Chosen by the god ✦17 writings · 30 lines

    Other kings said a god had chosen them. The Cyrus Cylinder says the lord of the gods, angry with Babylon, searched all lands for a righteous prince and took Cyrus by the hand, and Isaiah calls the same Cyrus the LORD’s anointed, whose right hand he has held. The Kushite Piye calls himself divine emanation, living image of Atum, and at Meroë the priests could send the king word from the gods that he must die. Ireland gave the choosing to a stone that the ruler must find on the last day of the assembly, and to the Sovereignty, loathsome at first and beautiful at last.

  5. 05 A man on a throne among the gods ✦7 writings · 14 lines

    The Jewish books know the claim from the inside. A hymn from Qumran has a man speak of a mighty throne in the congregation of the gods, on which none of the ancient kings shall sit, and ask who can be compared with him in his glory; another scroll reads Psalm 82 as the trial of Belial, with Melchizedek judging in the divine assembly. In 3 Enoch the man Enoch is called the Lesser YHWH over the angels’ protests, and when a visitor cried that there are two powers in heaven, he was given sixty lashes of fire. Ezekiel’s prince of Tyre says, I am a god, I sit in the seat of God in the midst of the seas, and is told, yet thou art a man.

  6. 06 Horus and Seth before the gods ✦11 writings · 20 lines

    Egypt put the throne itself on trial: in the Contendings of Horus and Seth, Seth is brought in fetters and the Sun-god takes him to dwell with him as his son, to thunder in the sky and be feared. The Shabaka Stone has Geb think it wrong that the portion of Horus should be like the portion of Seth. A Hyksos king chose Seth for his lord, while Hatshepsut reviled the foreigners who ruled without Re. Israel’s prophet later called Pharaoh the great dragon lying in the midst of his rivers.

  7. 07 The council on Olympus ✦10 writings · 19 lines

    Greece kept the gods’ own court. Pindar tells how Zeus and Poseidon strove for Thetis until Themis told the council she was fated to bear a son mightier than his father, and the Cypria says Zeus swore she should be the wife of a mortal. Thetis had once saved Zeus when Hera, Poseidon and Athena would have bound him, and the Cypria has Zeus plan the Trojan war with Themis. Zeus flung Hephaistos from the threshold of heaven and Ate from the starry sky, swearing she should never return, and an old Hittite rite remembers when the first gods were made the last.

  8. 08 The old gods driven out ✦5 writings · 12 lines

    When a new faith came the old gods were put out by name. In Kiev Vladimir had Perun dragged to the river at a horse’s tail and beaten to affront the demon, and the devil groaned that he was driven out by the ignorant, not by apostles. The men of Novgorod told the idol, Little Perun, you have drunk and eaten your fill, now float away, and Herberstein was told it swam against the stream while a voice was heard at the bridge. The Vendidad drives out Indra and the other daevas by name, and in Japan the star-god Kagase-wo alone refused to submit.

  9. 09 I am God and there is no other13 writings · 23 lines

    In Isaiah the LORD says there is no god beside him. The Gnostic books put the line in the mouth of the blind maker of this world, I am God and there is no other God beside me, and have his mother answer, Do not lie, Ialdabaoth. The Russian chronicle’s Sathanael says he will be like God and set his throne upon the northern clouds, and in the Life of Adam and Eve the elder angel blames Adam for his expulsion from the glory he had in the heavens. The Yezidi Book of Revelation speaks in the angel’s own voice: I was, am now, and shall have no end.

  10. 10 How art thou fallen, O Lucifer12 writings · 27 lines

    The name comes from Isaiah’s song over a king of Babylon, told as the fall of a star: in the Hebrew he is Helel son of Dawn, who said he would sit on the mount of meeting in the far recesses of Zaphon and is brought down to Sheol. The Greek Bible makes him the dawn-bringer who rose early and the Latin makes him Lucifer; the King James Bible prints the name with a capital, once. Ugarit knew the same mountain, where Athtar went up to Baal’s throne on Zaphon and his feet did not reach the footstool, and Ezekiel’s king of Tyre was a cherub in Eden, every precious stone his covering.

  11. 11 The morning star in the church ✦12 writings · 16 lines

    The church kept both meanings of the word: the Vulgate’s lucifer is the morning star itself in the Psalm and in Job, Second Peter waits for the day-star to rise in the heart, Revelation’s Jesus is the bright and morning star, and the Easter Exsultet hails the lucifer which knows no setting. Origen reads Isaiah as the fall of one who was once Lucifer and arose in the morning, and Tertullian calls him, before he became the devil, the wisest of creatures. Jerome found the Saracens of Elusa worshipping Venus for the sake of Lucifer, and a bishop named Lucifer gave his name to a party of the church. Dante climbs out of hell along the body of the creature who once was fair, and sees the planet of love making the whole east laugh.

  12. 12 Dawn’s son in the poets ✦13 writings · 30 lines

    Hesiod gives the star its birth: Dawn bore Eosphoros, the dawn-bringer, to Astraeus, son of a Titan. Homer sets the evening star, fairest in heaven, on Achilles’ spear, and Plato’s epigram on Aster has the boy shine as the morning star among the living and as Hesperus among the dead. Virgil’s Morning Star is the one Venus loves above all the stellar fires, while Ovid’s Lucifer fathers the peaceful king Ceyx. In China the poet Li Bai was named for the evening star, Changgeng, which entered his mother’s dream on the night of his birth.

  13. 13 The star of Inana, al-ʿUzzā and Quetzalcoatl ✦17 writings · 26 lines

    Sumer knew the star as Inana’s: Enheduana, the first author to sign her name, hails the lady of all the me, light come forth blazing, and a king of Isin praises the Dilbad star at evening. In Arabia the morning star was al-ʿUzzā, whom the Saracens worshipped as Venus and whom the Qurʾān names among the three that are only names. In Mexico Quetzalcoatl, driven from his kingdom, came to the eastern sea and burned himself, and his heart rose as the morning star. The Theosophists of 1913 had the Lords of the Flame come down from Venus in a chariot of fire.

  14. 14 The rebel’s side, told again ✦7 writings · 11 lines

    From the seventeenth century the poets let the fallen speak. Milton’s Satan says the mind is its own place and that it is better to reign in hell than serve in heaven, and Vondel’s Lucifer refuses the decree that sets man above the angels. Byron’s Lucifer has a victor but no superior, while Madách’s has lived from all eternity. Blake makes the Devil the original archangel, and Carducci hails Satan as the one for whom Adonis and Astarte lived.

  15. 15 He said he was Viracocha6 writings · 15 lines

    Some claimants were found out: in Peru Viracocha warned before he left that people would come saying they were Viracocha, and a servant came back to preach that he was the creator, until the people saw it was false. The Maya Seven Macaw puffed himself up as the sun before there was a sun, but he was not truly the sun, and what is left of him is a mirror. Simon the magician carried a woman from Tyre about as Helen, his first thought and mother of all, and had himself worshipped in the likeness of Jupiter. The Mandaeans warn of a Christ clothed in fire who says, I am God, and is not Hibil-Ziwa.

  16. 16 The gods of the Arabs, given back2 writings · 5 lines

    Arabia’s record is cut in stone. Esarhaddon of Assyria says he refurbished six captured gods of the Arabs, Atar-samayin among them, and gave them back with the might of Aššur and his own name inscribed on them. Over the Arabs he set the lady Tabūʾa, who was raised in his father’s palace, and a rebel was put in neck stocks at the gate. Herodotus describes the Arabian pledge, sworn by their gods Orotalt and Alilat.

  17. 17 Obodas the king, whom they make a god4 writings · 9 lines

    At Petra and in the Negev the Nabataeans made a king a god. Stephanus of Byzantium, quoting Uranius, records the place where Obodas the king, whom they make a god, lies buried. A Petra inscription remembers the banqueting-society of Obodat the god, and at the spring of ʿAyn ʿAvdat people swore as Obodat lives. At Miletus Syllaios, brother of the king, made a dedication for the life of King Obodas.

  18. 18 Who holds the gods holds the water3 writings · 10 lines

    In Yemen the gods and the water went together. A Sabaean king besieged a rival city by order of ʿAthtar, seized it for Almaqah and for Saba, cut its land off from the water and made it build a temple of Almaqah in its midst. Centuries later the Christian ruler Abraha opened his inscription on the Maʾrib dam with the Merciful, his Messiah and the Holy Spirit, then told how the dam and its sluices broke and the tribes were summoned to seal the breaches. Another Sabaean ruler, Yathaʿamar Watar, made a dedication when he brought his patron god Aranyadaʿ home.

  19. 19 Descended from Troy ✦23 writings · 40 lines

    Peoples claimed descent through the house of Troy. Snorri’s Prologue puts Troy at the centre of the earth and makes Thor a grandson of Priam, whose people went north and were taken for gods, and Geoffrey sends Brutus the Trojan to an island held by giants. The Persians traced their name to Perseus’s son Perses, and the Ethiopian royal house in Heliodorus counted the Sun, Perseus, Andromeda and Memnon among its ancestors. Rome fetched the Mother of the Gods from Phrygia when the Sibylline books called for her, the goddess who had almost followed Aeneas’s ships, and the Irish put Fénius Farsaid where Isidore had put Magog, as father of the Scythians and the Goths.

  20. 20 The losers’ book travelled6 writings · 12 lines

    The losers’ side of the story survives because its books travelled. The Book of Giants, which tells the fallen angels’ and their sons’ side, went from the Dead Sea caves to the followers of Mani; a Parthian letter says a brother has taken it with him and another copy was made at Merv. A Chinese list of Mani’s books calls it the Sūtra of the Mighty Warriors, and the church banned the book about the giant Ogias who fought a dragon after the flood. Augustine, nine years a hearer among the Manichaeans, reports their teaching that particles of God stay bound in the fruit until the elect release them by eating.

  21. 21 Its witnesses hide their faces8 writings · 19 lines

    Some books were written down in hiding: the Popol Vuh says the original book exists, but its witnesses and those who ponder it hide their faces. An old man of Cholula, a hundred years of age, told the friar Durán to take ink and paper, and Sahagún gathered the oldest men of Tlatelolco to set down what they knew. The Kebra Nagast tells how Zion was carried off from Jerusalem, so that the daughters of Zion were rejected and the daughters of Ethiopia honoured, and says it went untranslated under the Zagwe kings. The Mande griots call themselves the keepers of the oaths the ancestors swore.

  22. 22 The book that came down from heaven ✦24 writings · 39 lines

    Many archives say their oldest book came from the gods. Edfu’s builders followed a book that descended from the sky north of Memphis, and the prince Setne went looking for the book Thoth wrote with his own hand. Berossus has Xisuthrus bury all writings at Sippar before the flood, while the Book of Sothis claims inscriptions traced in sacred language by Thoth and translated after it. Euhemerus said he found a golden column on which Zeus wrote his own deeds, and Blavatsky said an archaic manuscript of palm leaves lay before her eyes.

  23. 23 The historian’s own sight ✦25 writings · 46 lines

    Some writers weighed the claims and said so. Philo of Byblos says Hesiod and the cyclic poets made up their theogonies and with them defeated the truth, and Herodotus marks where his own sight, judgement and inquiry end and the Egyptians’ report begins. The priest of Saïs tells Solon the Greeks are all children, and Stesichorus recants: that story is not true. Movses of Armenia, weighing the fables, decides that much of them is true, and Sima Qian, of the clan that once cut heaven from earth, set the most refined of the old words at the head of his history.

  24. 24 Every history from Tuan ✦4 writings · 10 lines

    Ireland kept its record in living witnesses. A slaughter is not usual without a tale-bearer escaping from it, says Tuan, and that man am I; every history and every pedigree in Ireland is from Tuan. God kept Fintan alive till the time of the saints, and the Senchas Már names what has preserved the tradition of the men of Ireland: the joint memory of two elders, the passing from one ear to another, the chanting of poets and the law of letters.

  25. 25 The gatherers read the sky ✦6 writings · 13 lines

    Modern readers gathered the tellings and read them as a record of the sky. Santillana and von Dechend read the myths as the slow shifting of the sun through the signs, so that time alone turns the Titans into workers of iniquity. Velikovsky wrote of wars among the planets in historical times, and Clube and Napier took the giants for comets. Sitchin made the Sumerian šar an orbit of 3,600 years and the Anunnaki those who from heaven to earth came, and Hancock reads the shared myths as a legacy from a single, remotely ancient source.

  26. 26 Chosen by the whole people6 writings · 21 lines

    The Buddhist account of the first king gives the opposite of a god-king. When stealing, censure, lying and punishment had appeared, the people chose the handsomest among them and paid him in rice: chosen by the whole people is what is meant by Mahā Sammata, the Great Elect, and the Buddha adds that kings came from among those very beings and no others. The Greek writers made their first kings men who became gods: Diodorus’s Uranus gathered men into walled cities and was honoured after death, and Philo’s Phoenicians deified the Most High. Atlantis’s ten kings judged one another at night by Poseidon’s law, and the Norse god Rig came striding from the grove to teach a boy runes and claim him as his son.

  27. 27 The rank, and not the life17 writings · 38 lines

    Every claimant in this part gets the rank, and not the life: Aqhat is offered deathlessness and answers, yes, I shall surely die. The ale-wife tells Gilgamesh that when the gods made mankind they kept life for themselves, and a snake takes his plant of youth. Psalm 82 tells the gods they shall die like men, and the Hermetic Key says man on earth is a god subject to death, while no god of heaven comes down. In China the Investiture of the Gods gives the war’s dead their offices as true gods, a rank in heaven for those who lost their lives.

  1. 01 For seventy generations in the valleys of the earth7 writings · 17 lines

    The losers of the first age were bound rather than destroyed, and almost every binding has a term. Enoch’s angels are to be bound fast for seventy generations in the valleys of the earth, till the day of their judgement, and 2 Enoch says they are condemned to be under the earth till heaven and earth are ended. In Enoch’s vision the stars that did not rise at their appointed time are bound for ten thousand years. The Zohar keeps Aza and Azael in the mountains of darkness, where those who seek them are taught sorceries, and Mujāhid, in al-Ṭabarī’s commentary, hangs Hārūt and Mārūt upside-down in chains of iron.

  2. 02 The giants’ prison ✦4 writings · 12 lines

    Mani’s books give the giants’ side of the sentence. The Kephalaia says a prison was made for the Watchers below the mountains before they ever rebelled, with cities for the giants’ children, who would live a thousand years, and in the Book of Giants they are led to towns at the foot of Mount Sumeru, where the giant Sāhm and his brother will live eternally. Pistis Sophia binds the rebel rulers to a turning sphere, six months facing one way and six the other. The Apocryphon of John says fate was begotten to bind them with measures and times and moments.

  3. 03 His time hath not yet come9 writings · 21 lines

    Iran’s dragon-king Zahhāk, Azi Dahak in the old books, was bound and not killed. When Faridun would have struck, the angel Surush cried, Strike not, his time hath not yet come, and Faridun chained him in a narrow gorge of Damāvand. Al-Ṭabarī says the Persians claim he is there to this day, fettered with iron, and they kept the day he was tied up as a feast. At the nine-thousandth year, says the Bahman Yasht, he breaks loose, and the hero Keresaspa sleeps in the plain of Peshyansai under the glory of heaven, guarded by a myriad spirits, so that when the dragon is unfettered he may arise and slay him.

  4. 04 Nine thousand years, and the making new ✦8 writings · 17 lines

    Iran set the whole contest on a clock. In the Bundahishn Ohrmazd, knowing how it would end, offers the evil spirit peace under a term of nine thousand years, and the Bahman Yasht shows Zarathustra a tree of seven metal branches, the seven ages to come. At the end the metal of the hills melts into a river through which all men pass and are made pure, and the saviour Saoshyant restores the world. Plutarch heard from the Persians of two rival gods, good and evil, and the Vendidad’s commentators say the people in Yima’s enclosure never die.

  5. 05 The bound Loki and the wolf3 writings · 16 lines

    The Norse gods bound Loki, the trickster, and his son the wolf until the end of the world, since they would not stain their holy place with the wolf’s blood. Fenrir was bound with a fetter made of six things, the noise of a cat’s footfall and the beard of a woman among them, and Týr lost his hand in the wolf’s mouth. Loki was bound over three stones with his own son’s entrails, while a serpent drips venom on his face and his wife holds a basin under it. At the end the sea gushes over the land as the Midgard Serpent stirs, and afterwards Lif and Lifthrasir come out of Hoddmimir’s wood to people the world.

  6. 06 The power that sleeps in the rock ✦6 writings · 15 lines

    The lands east of the Baltic kept their bound and buried strength. The Kalevala says power is not lost in the caves of the earth though the wizards die, and Väinämöinen sails away in a copper boat saying men will look for him and miss him, and he will be needed again. Kalevipoeg sits on horseback at the gate of hell with his hand fettered to the rock, and his chain grows as thin as a hair at Christmas, until one day a flame will cut his hand free and Kalev will come home. In Russia the giant Svyatogor lies down in a coffin and cannot lift the lid, and the heroes who called for a host not of this world turned to stone in the mountains.

  7. 07 Iron pellets and molten copper11 writings · 16 lines

    China’s stone monkey Sun Wukong, who stole immortality from heaven, lies under a mountain more than five hundred years, fed on iron pellets and molten copper until the day of his calamity is full and someone comes to save him. Gun was shut away for ever at Feather Mountain, and the Tianwen asks why for three years he was not let go. Shun cast the four criminals out to the four borders of the world. Kashmir’s dragon asks that when the Buddha’s law has ended it may return to dwell in its country as a lake, and the Mongols waited four hundred years in Ergene Qun until they melted the iron mountain.

  8. 08 As long as there is sun and light2 writings · 8 lines

    The Maya hero twins bound the earthquake-giant Cabracan, who swore, I will wreck them as long as there is sun and light, and buried him in the earth, and the mountain settled on his brother Zipacna’s chest until he became stone. In Peru Viracocha had the disobedient servant Taguapaca tied hand and foot and set adrift on the lake, threatening that he would return and take vengeance. Ayar Cachi, the strong brother, was sealed in the cave of origins by a rock set against its window, and his jailer was turned to stone, which they still show today.

  9. 09 In the stocks until the end of the worlds3 writings · 6 lines

    The Mandaeans, a small faith of Iraq whose maker of the world is a lesser power, say Abathur in his anger set the maker Ptahil in the stocks until the end of the worlds, when the two will forgive one another; Jōšamin rose from his throne and called up sixty-one fetters round him. The Ginza counts the destructions still to come, and the priests told Ethel Drower of a fourth destruction, by wind, in the 791st year of the sign of the Fish. The Yezidis say that in every thousand years one of the seven gods comes down to set the laws and then returns.

  10. 10 Bound in the deep ✦10 writings · 20 lines

    Greece bound its losers below: Hesiod puts the Titans in Tartarus behind a fence of bronze, and Prometheus in inextricable bonds with a shaft driven through him. Aeschylus’s Prometheus says he will escape his bondage only after tortures infinite, and he is told to look for no end of his agony until some god takes his woes upon himself of his own free will. Apollodorus says Prometheus offered himself to Zeus to be immortal in Chiron’s stead, and Andromeda, bound to a rock for the sea-monster, was set among the stars beneath her mother. Dante found the giants chained around the pit of hell, and the emperor of the dolorous kingdom standing in the ice from mid-breast up.

  11. 11 The Thigh of Seth chained to the pole ✦9 writings · 17 lines

    Egypt kept its bound enemy in the sky. The Book of the Day says the Thigh of Seth is in the northern sky, tied to two pegs of flint by a chain of gold and guarded by Isis as a hippopotamus, and at Thebes the rite of overthrowing Apep was repeated daily in the temple of Amen-Re, at the beginning of every hour. In the Pyramid Texts the king’s bones are iron and his limbs the imperishable stars, and Osiris, fallen on his side at Nedit, is told to raise himself, receive his head, collect his bones and gather his limbs. In the Book of the Dead, Atum says he will destroy all he has made, and the earth will return to the primordial water.

  12. 12 A door closed by a single failure12 writings · 16 lines

    Some doors to deathlessness were closed by a single failure. In Japan the heavenly grandson sent back the Rock Princess, and she cursed him: had he taken her, his children would have endured for ever like the massy rocks. Dawn won deathlessness for Tithonus but did not think to ask youth for him, and Zeus would not give Attis back his life, granting only that his body should not decay and his least finger should move. In India Nahuṣa, fallen for his pride, must wander ten thousand years as a great serpent, and the Narts, offered eternal life or eternal glory, chose glory.

  13. 13 Our years are bound1 writings · 20 lines

    In Mexico every fifty-two years the count of the years was bound up, and every fire was put out and drilled anew. On the night of the new fire everyone was in very great fear, for if no fire could be made the sun would not rise and the demons would come down to eat them. Pregnant women were masked with maguey and shut in the granaries, and children were kept awake lest they turn into mice. When the fire was drilled on a captive’s breast the towns blazed until it seemed to be day, and Sahagún’s informants said the count of the years was very old, invented by Quetzalcoatl.

  14. 14 Once a year ✦5 writings · 7 lines

    Some bindings come round on the calendar. Quintus says the river by Memnon’s grave turns to blood once a year on the day he died, and at Thebes his broken statue gave out a sound at dawn. The reeds that grew where Midas’s barber buried his master’s secret repeated it about twelve months later, and the birds of Memnon war again each time the sun has passed through the twelve signs. Hesiod times the harvest by the rising of the Pleiades.

  15. 15 In a year called One Reed12 writings · 16 lines

    Some wait to return rather than to break loose. Quetzalcoatl left saying he would come back in a year called One Reed, to visit them when it was time for the world to end, and in another telling he burned himself by the eastern sea and his heart rose as the morning star. The Hopi elder brother went toward the rising sun with one of the two stone tablets, to bring it back when he hears the desperate call for aid, and Wagadu, four times lost, will be found again. The morning star itself is the one who returns: the Vulgate promises that you shall arise as the morning star, and Dante, climbing out of hell, sees the planet of love making the east laugh.

  16. 16 Do the ravens still fly round the mountain?16 writings · 21 lines

    Some sleep under the hill until they are needed. The Grimms tell of the emperor in the mountain who wakes to ask whether the ravens still fly round it, and Malory gives Arthur’s tomb its verse, king once and king to be. Armenia’s Little Mher, made deathless by a curse, waits in the Raven’s Rock until the world is built again and the ground can hold him. Ireland buried King Lóegaire standing on the heights of Tara like a man in battle, face to face with his enemies until the day of judgement, and in Wales the buried head of Bran kept every invader from the island while it lay hidden.

  17. 17 Kronos asleep on his island ✦14 writings · 21 lines

    At the rim of the world the elder king sleeps. Plutarch was told that Kronos is confined by Zeus, his sleep devised as a bondage for him, and that he sees in his dreams all that Zeus premeditates; Philo gives Kronos four eyes, two waking and two asleep. Hesiod says Kronos rules over the heroes Zeus set apart at the ends of the earth, and Thetis carried Achilles from the pyre to the White Island in the Euxine, where he rules and Helen was wedded to him. The long-lived Ethiopians bound their prisoners with fetters of gold and told the Persian king’s spies they had stronger chains than these.

  18. 18 Until Gog and Magog are opened14 writings · 20 lines

    The scriptures keep the same rule of a term. Daniel’s watcher sentences a king to live as a beast until seven times pass, and the horn that cast down the stars holds the saints only for an appointed time. Alexander shut the peoples of the north behind gates of iron, and the Qurʾān says the wall stands until the promise of the Lord comes and Gog and Magog are opened. The rabbis give the world six thousand years, and Ireland’s Morrígan foresees a world she will not love, summer without blossoms, cattle without milk.

  19. 19 The great year turns ✦16 writings · 31 lines

    The term can also be the turning of the whole sky. Censorinus reports the greatest year, whose winter is a flood and whose summer a fire, and the Etruscan ages, each closed by portents; Plutarch says a trumpet sounded from a clear sky at the end of one of them. Virgil’s souls are summoned in a throng to the river when time’s wheel has rolled through a thousand years, and the Hermetic Asclepius promises a rebirth of the world that makes all good things new. Mani’s books end with light returned to the great light, and Babylon’s Marduk says of the craftsmen he sent down to the Apsû, I have not yet decreed their return.

  20. 20 The witnesses who cannot die12 writings · 29 lines

    Some stayed alive through all of it: Utnapishtim and his wife were made like gods after the flood, and Berossus has Xisuthrus taken up to dwell with the gods. In 3 Enoch, Enoch says he was removed from the generation of the flood to be a witness against them in the high heavens, and the Ethiopian Synaxarium says Elijah and Enoch shall come together in the last days. In Ireland God kept Fintan alive under the Flood so that he could tell the Takings of Ireland, and Tuan, who had been a hawk and a boar, says, That man am I. The Sibyl, bride of Noah’s son, left the walls of Babylon to prophesy to the Greeks, and Virgil’s Sibyl tells Aeneas the descent to Avernus is easy but the way back is the toil.

  21. 21 The period being ended2 writings · 5 lines

    The Hermetic Poimandres gives the rule of every binding: the period being ended, the bond that bound them all was loosened by God’s will. Its Man, though deathless and above the Harmony, suffers as a mortal and has become a slave within it. In the Virgin of the World, Hermes sets a spell on his hidden books before he returns to heaven, and the Monarch comes to hear the souls’ request.

  1. 01 The records keep their own clocks10 writings · 11 lines

    The records keep their own clocks: the rabbis give the world six thousand years, and the Zohar opens the gates of wisdom in the six hundredth year of the sixth millennium. The Bundahishn counts twelve thousand years in all, and the Bahman Yasht seven ages of metal. The Indian astronomer Āryabhaṭa counted the sun’s revolutions in an age and dated his work from the Bhārata war, the war in which Kṛṣṇa says, in Oppenheimer’s English, Now I am become Death, the destroyer of worlds. Darius counts eight of his race as kings before him, and the First Emperor of China counted his line forward to ten thousand generations.

  2. 02 Nine thousand years back to Atlantis4 writings · 11 lines

    Plato’s Egyptian priest of Saïs, who told Solon that the Greeks were never anything but children, counted nine thousand years back to the war with Atlantis, and said many great deluges had come within those years. He said Athens was founded a thousand years before Saïs, and that Solon heard Greek names for foreign kings because the names had been translated as they crossed. Herodotus was told that in the long count of Egypt’s kings the sun had four times risen contrary to custom, twice rising where it now sets.

  3. 03 A natural age is the longest life3 writings · 11 lines

    The Etruscans held that their nation had been given a set number of ages, each ended by a sign from heaven. Censorinus, writing in AD 238, defines the measure: a natural age is the longest duration of human life, bounded by birth and death. The portents were written down, and Rome took its hundred-year age from the Etruscans. Plutarch says a trumpet rang out of a clear sky at the end of one Etruscan age, and the haruspex Vulcanius told the assembly that Caesar’s comet marked the end of the ninth age and the start of the tenth.

  4. 04 The morning star’s return5 writings · 7 lines

    Some dates the records have already fixed. The Maya Dresden Codex brings the Venus year of 584 days into step with the sun’s in eight years, 2920 days, and this volume’s own ephemeris finds the same eight-year return: the morning star passed between the earth and the sun on 26 October 2018 and does so again on 24 October 2026. The same reckoning puts the owner’s natal Venus at 8°52′ Capricorn, an evening star. Nonnus dated the flood by the stars, each set apart in its own house, with Kronos passing over the rainy back of Capricorn.

  5. 05 The calendar keeps the door ✦13 writings · 28 lines

    The calendars keep their doors: in Mexico every fifty-two years made one bundle, and when the new fire was drilled the people rejoiced that pestilence and hunger had passed. At Thebes the colossus of Memnon gave out a sound at dawn, though Strabo could not say whether it came from the stone or from a man standing by. Egypt’s priests overthrew Apep daily at the beginning of every hour, and in the Book of Nut, Geb calls the sky goddess the sow who eats her piglets, for swallowing the stars. The Persians kept the day Zahhāk was bound as the feast of Mihrajan, and Kalevipoeg’s chain thins at Christmas.

  6. 06 The house in which this tablet is placed15 writings · 18 lines

    The Erra poem ends with a promise to the house that keeps its tablet: the singer who chants it shall not die in pestilence, and the scribe who masters it shall be spared in an enemy land. The Cuthaean Legend tells its reader to open the tablet box, and the Narts say that though they all perish the harp will live for ever and tell of them. An old man of Cholula told Durán to take ink and paper, and the Mande griots call themselves the keepers of the oaths the ancestors swore. This volume means to be that kind of record, keeping what the archives say and marking what it joined.

  7. 07 The record as argument ✦20 writings · 26 lines

    Records were also used to win. Euhemerus said Zeus wrote his own deeds on a golden column, and Philo answered that Hesiod and the cyclic poets made up theogonies with which they defeated the truth. Xerxes reminded the Argives that his forefather Perses was the son of Perseus, and Rome sent for the Mother of the Gods because the Sibylline books said she must come. Free cities set up statues of Marsyas as the sign of their freedom, and Keating quotes Boece’s verse that wherever the Stone of Destiny is found the Scots will reign.
