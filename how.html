How it was made

The rules first, then the numbers. Every number is counted from the files when this page is built.

The rules

Everything is canon. Every people’s telling is read as a telling of the one story. Where two records differ, both are kept as variants. Nothing is disproved, and nothing is dismissed for sounding outrageous.

Every line is a real line. A quotation is matched character for character against the ledger, which holds the passage, its text, its translator and its date. A line that has not yet been checked against the printed book is printed in italics and says so. A line we translated ourselves says so.

The joins are marked. Where the book links two records that no old author linked, the text says the link is ours and marks it ◇. Ancient authors’ own equations (Philo saying Elos is Kronos; Pseudo-Eupolemus saying Atlas is Enoch) are given as theirs, with their names.

Readers who know nothing read it first. Three readers who know none of the archives read each chapter for stumbles, and a checker tests every date and identification added by the drafter. In Chapter I about a quarter of the added context needed correcting, and in Chapter II about a fifth; the corrections were made before each chapter went out.

The full method, the grades and the relation types are in Method; the house rules for the prose in Style; the state of the work in the Handoff.

Everything below is counted from the files when this page is built. Nothing here was typed by hand.

How it was checked

Every quotation in the story and the chapters is matched character for character against the ledger by quotecheck.py; a flagged record is refused until the printed source is opened, unless the chapter declares it a house rendering. housecheck.py holds the prose to the house rules. A panel of three readers who know none of the archives reads each chapter for stumbles, and a checker tests every date and identification the drafter added.
