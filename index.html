Bhāravataraṇa · the unburdening

One story, told by the world's oldest records

Bhāravataraṇa is a Sanskrit word from the Mahābhārata: the lifting of the earth's burden. This book lays the oldest records of many peoples end to end and finds that they tell one story. A door between heaven and earth stood open. Things came through it. The earth grew too heavy. Remedies were tried. The door was shut, and someone still waits behind it.

Begin here: The Horse's Foot →

Eleven parts, about 19 minutes to read in order. Under them, 1,664 quoted lines from 26 peoples and 197 named texts, 1,269 figures, and the chapters at full height, 2 of 11 written.

Nothing here is invented. Every line is quoted from a named translation and checked against it, character for character. Where this book links two records that no old author linked, it says so and marks the join ◇. Where records differ, both are kept; nothing is disproved.

The story, in eleven parts

Read them in order. Each part gives what the records say in a page, then every people that tells it, with its own lines.

  • prologueThe Horse's Foot
    At Van, a hero waits behind a door in the cliff for ground that will bear his horse. The oldest records say the earth grew too heavy; this book asks how.
    told by 23 peoples · 265 lines
  • IThe Elders
    Before the present gods there were elders. The younger threw them down and kept them: asleep, sworn by, under the ground, or made into it.
    told by 23 peoples · 287 lines
  • IIGoing Down
    In the first age the way between heaven and earth stood open. Kingship came down it, and teachers, and kings on a cord, and shining beings who lost their light.
    told by 25 peoples · 267 lines
  • IIIThe Gifts of the Smiths
    Everything that makes a civilisation came through the door: the metals, writing, the stars. One record calls it a gift; the next calls it a crime.
    told by 24 peoples · 337 lines
  • IVHybrids
    The beings who came down took wives among the people below. Their children were the heroes and the giants.
    told by 21 peoples · 211 lines
  • VThe Burden
    Then the earth could not carry it. Babylon says too many people; Genesis says wickedness; India says the kings themselves.
    told by 19 peoples · 158 lines
  • VIRemedy
    The first remedy was water, the flood. The second was war: Troy and Kurukṣetra stand where the flood stood.
    told by 22 peoples · 335 lines
  • VIIThe Cut
    The door was shut. China sent two officers to end the traffic between heaven and earth; Tibet's king cut his own cord; Enoch's angels may never go back up.
    told by 23 peoples · 229 lines
  • VIIIClaimed
    After the shutting, kings claim they came through the door, and the books say who really did. Every claimant gets the rank and not the life.
    told by 25 peoples · 536 lines
  • IXAwaiting
    The losers of the first age were bound rather than destroyed, and almost every binding has a term. Some wait to come back.
    told by 25 peoples · 351 lines
  • epilogueWhere We Are Now
    The records keep their own clocks, and this year can be set on all of them. The door at Van opens next on the eve of Ascension.
    told by 19 peoples · 112 lines
