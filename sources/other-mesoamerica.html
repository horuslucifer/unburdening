in the story The Horse's Foot · Awaiting · Where We Are Now · far_shores-0077

When the said wheel of the years was finished, at the beginning of the new one, which was called Two Reed [umeacatl], the people of Mexico and of all the region used to hold a great feast or ceremony which they called toximmolpilia, which is as it were the binding of the years; and this ceremony was held every fifty-two years, that is, after each of the four signs had ruled the years thirteen times. That feast was called toximmolpilia, which means: our years are bound; and because it was the beginning of another twelve [sic]. They also said xiuhtzitzquilo, which means: the new year is taken; and in sign of this each one touched the grasses, to give to understand that the count of another twelve [sic] years was now beginning, so that a hundred and four might be completed, which make an age.

So at that time they also drew new fire; and when the day appointed for drawing it drew near, every householder of Mexico used to throw into the water, the canals or the lagoons, the stones or sticks they held as the gods of their house, and also the stones of their hearths for cooking food and those with which they ground chiles; and they cleaned their houses very well, and at the end they killed all the fires. A certain place was appointed where the new fire was drawn and made, and an altar, on top of a hill called Huixachtlan, which is at the bounds of the towns of Iztapalapa and Colhuacan, two leagues from Mexico; and the fire was made at midnight, and the stick from which the fire was drawn was set on the breast of a captive taken in war, the most noble one.
