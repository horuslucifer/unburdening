"Twelve hundred years had not yet gone by. The land spread wide. The people grew many. The land roared like a bull. At their din the god was troubled. Enlil heard their noise. He spoke to the great gods: "The noise of mankind has grown too great for me. Their din will not let me sleep." *[Atra-hasis I 352ff. House rendering. Editors read the number as 1200 or 600. Noise as overpopulation: Kilmer 1972; as revolt: Oden 1981.]*

“The land had become great; the people had multiplied. / The land like a bull had become satiated. / [In] their assemblage God was absent. / ... heard their clamor. / He said to the great gods (?) / Those observing the clamor of men." (Text A, col. I 2-7, in Clay's "Selection from the early version") Colophon: "The second tablet (of the series) "When / God, man." / Its total is 439 (lines) / By the hand of Azag-dAya, the junior / scribe. / Month Shebet, day 28th / of the year when Ammi-zaduga, the king, / built the city Dûr-Ammi-zaduga / at the mouth / of the Euphrates (11th year)." Clay's introduction: "It was copied from a still earlier inscription by a junior scribe named Azag-Aya, on the 28th day of Shebet, in the 11th year of Ammi-zaduga (1966 B.C.) ... The original from which the scribe copied had already been injured in the 12th line, which is indicated by the word hibis " broken."”

F10. Clay 1922, the Morgan tablet (Old Babylonian Atra-ḫasīs, Tablet II): the noise, and the dated colophon. Public domain.
