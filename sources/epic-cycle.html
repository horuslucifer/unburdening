“‘There was a time when the countless tribes of men, though wide-dispersed, oppressed the surface of the deep-bosomed earth, and Zeus saw it and had pity and in his wise heart resolved to relieve the all-nurturing earth of men by causing the great struggle of the Ilian war, that the load of death might empty the world. And so the heroes were slain in Troy, and the plan of Zeus came to pass.’”

F17. The Cypria verses, as the scholiast quotes them: number, pity, the load of death.

Others said that Homer spoke from a certain story. For they say that the earth, weighed down by the multitude of men, there being no piety among men, asked Zeus to be lightened of the load; and Zeus first made the Theban war at once, through which he destroyed very many; and afterwards again he took Momos for his counsellor (this is what Homer calls the plan of Zeus), since he was able to destroy them all with thunderbolts or with floods. Momos prevented this, and put to him two things instead: the marriage of Thetis to a mortal, and the begetting of a beautiful daughter. From these two came war for Greeks and barbarians, and from that time it came about that the earth was lightened, many being done away with. The story is in Stasinus, who made the Cypria, who says thus: (house) "And again the goddess murky Night, though she lay with none, bare Blame and painful Woe"

F18. The scholion's own prose (house): Earth's petition, the impiety, Momos's two proposals; and Hesiod's Momos, Night's fatherless child. our translation
